Gluing Local Contexts into Global Meaning: A Sheaf-Theoretic Decomposition of Transformer Representations

Bryce Grant, Peng Wang
Case Western Reserve University
NeurIPS 2026
Paper arXiv Code

Abstract

Prior research into interpretability does not test whether a representation stays consistent under paraphrasing. Each pair of paraphrases can be thought of as a local witness that two activations should be the same. From these local witnesses we can build a cellular sheaf on the paraphrase graph. The question of what is consistent under paraphrasing can be answered by the Laplacian \(L_\mathcal{F}\) given a frozen model. No training required. Our kernel \(H^0\) gives us a content subspace. We show that with only 20 dims the kernel outperforms the subspace that LEACE retains with all dims on held-out CounterFact fact retrieval by \(+17.1\) pp on average across 8 models. By constraining all paraphrases of a fact together we see an additional increase of \(+6.5\) pp. Our spectral complement shows us where the causal influence is concentrated. Ablating the coordinates that vary the most when paraphrased (which approximates ablating the spectral complement at the coordinate level) leads to \(5.6\) to \(26.5\times\) larger changes in model outputs compared to ablating coordinates with matched per-coordinate variability. We find this across 9 models from 124M to 13B params. By stacking layers we can create a grid which has \(b_1 = N(L-1)\) algebraic 1-cycles. The harmonic mass derived from the model varies by nearly 3 orders of magnitude between the 6 architectures. It also tracks the order of steering fragility (Spearman's \(\rho = 0.90\), exact one-sided permutation \(p = 0.014\)). The kernel, its spectral complement and the harmonic mass all come from the same paraphrase graph.

Method

Each node holds a sentence's mean-pooled hidden state, there is an edge between each pair of paraphrases, and one restriction map \(P \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}\) projects every pair to a common \(k\)-dimensional space. Taking the coboundary over an edge, \((\delta^0 s)_e = P^\top s_{v_2} - P^\top s_{v_1}\), gives a vector representing the degree of discrepancy between the two projected nodes. The sheaf Laplacian \(L_\mathcal{F} = (\delta^0)^\top \delta^0\) then splits \(\mathbb{R}^k\) into \(H^0 = \ker \delta^0\), given by the bottom eigenvectors, and the \(H^1\) directions, given by the top eigenvectors. Since this is only a \(k \times k\) eigenproblem (\(k = 128\)), it requires comparable compute for models ranging from 124M to 13B parameters.

Figure 1. Hidden states \(h_a, h_b\) pass through \(P^\top\) into edge space, where the coboundary \(\delta^0\) measures their disagreement. The sheaf Laplacian splits that space into \(H^0\), content stable across phrasing, and \(H^1\), context-dependent variation.

In the schematic above, the black arrow is the coboundary \(\delta^0 = P^\top(h_b - h_a)\), split into its \(H^0\) (blue) and \(H^1\) (red) components. Paraphrases with the same content give a \(\delta^0\) with no \(H^0\) component, since \(H^0 = \ker \delta^0\).

A single-layer matching graph has no closed loops, hence algebraic \(H^1 = 0\), and the Laplacian simply represents the projected covariance of differences within pairs. There are two sources of closed loops. First, stacking the same activations across layers yields \(b_1 = N(L-1)\) closed squares, over which to ask if going from layer \(l\) to layer \(l{+}1\) is consistent with moving between equivalent sentences. The Hodge harmonic mass of a model-derived edge cochain on this grid is the fragility diagnostic in the results below. Second, connecting more of a fact's paraphrases will alter \(H^0\) itself. We show one CounterFact fact under four topologies, and perform held-out hard retrieval with \(H^0\) built on each. The five-edge path recovers the gain over pairs (\(+7.7\) pp), and adding the closed loop improves by a further \(+0.5\) pp; hence the gain comes from constraining all edges jointly. Above each graph, \(b_0\) counts connected components and \(b_1\) independent cycles. Note \(b_1 = 0\) for the five-edge path.

    Results

    \(H^0\) beats LEACE on held-out fact retrieval

    For 20 dimensions, we compare sheaf \(H^0\) to the subspace preserved by LEACE on ROME CounterFact. We train on 70% of the 500 facts and evaluate on the remaining 30%. All eight models retrieve more hard same-relation examples when restricted to the 20-dimensional sheaf \(H^0\) than when restricted to the LEACE-preserved subspace at full hidden dimension, by an average of \(+17.1\) pp. This is largely unchanged with other choices of \(P\), including random maps and the identity, indicating that the content lives in the kernel of the within-pair difference covariance.

    Figure 2. Held-out hard CounterFact retrieval, where the Full and LEACE bars coincide because LEACE's edit does not change retrieval here. Sheaf \(H^0\) at 20 dimensions beats full-dimension LEACE-preserved on all eight models, by \(+1.5\) to \(+42.2\) pp (mean \(+17.1\) pp).

    Steering depends on the architecture

    Random steering directions with \(\alpha = 0.3\) are very destructive on Llama-2-7B: generation collapses and only 4.2% of facts are preserved. Directed methods are substantially more successful, retaining between 12% and 31% of facts, with the best performance for the contrastive sheaf matched to the model architecture (31.0%, McNemar \(p < 10^{-70}\) against random). For Mistral-7B, the best numerical performance is for the CCA sheaf, a statistical tie with random directions (41.6% vs. 39.4%, McNemar \(p = 0.19\)). For Llama-3-8B, random directions and PIXEL do best (36.0–38.8%).

    Figure 3. Fact preservation under five steering methods on CounterFact at \(\alpha = 0.3\) (\(n = 1000\) per model). On fragile Llama-2-7B, random steering collapses to 4.2% and the contrastive sheaf leads at 31.0%, while on Mistral-7B the CCA sheaf and random tie (41.6% vs. 39.4%).

    Harmonic mass and steering fragility

    These harmonic masses depend only on layer-transition differences for MRPC sentences, whereas fragility is measured on completely different facts from CounterFact, so the two are disjoint measurements of the same model. For the latter we steer along a random direction, scaled to 0.3 times the norm of the hidden state at layer \(\lfloor 5L/8 \rfloor\), and measure the fraction of CounterFact facts preserved. The three models with the lowest harmonic mass are the three most robust, and the three highest are the three most fragile (Spearman \(\rho = 0.90\), exact one-sided \(p = 0.014\)). Only Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B switch order (34.3% and 36.0% respectively, within sampling error).
